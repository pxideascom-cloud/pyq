INFERENCE Four kinds of demand, assigned by rule: numerical if the paper marks it NAT or the stem asks for a value to be computed; else application if it supplies a figure, data or a field scenario; else conceptual if it asks which statements hold or asks two lists to be matched; else theoretical recall. The rule is keyword-based, so individual questions can be misfiled, but the year-on-year proportions are stable enough to compare.

10. Preparation strategy

INFERENCE Everything below follows from the tables above; nothing new is assumed. Topics are taken in order of expected marks until half the paper is accounted for, then to three quarters.

The first 6 technical topics carry about half the paper's marks and the next 8 take it to three quarters. General Aptitude adds a further 10 marks and is the cheapest block on the paper to secure, since it needs no geology at all.

How to practise

EVIDENCE 29% of questions need a number computed, so timed formula work matters more than reading. MSQ appears 62 times in total and 41 times in the last three papers; each needs every option judged independently with no partial credit, so practise them as four true/false decisions rather than one choice. Structural Geology and Mineralogy carry the most figures, so diagram reading — stereonets, Miller indices, cross-sections — is worth drilling separately from theory.

SPECULATIVE Caveat on all of the above: topic labels come from keyword classification, so some questions sit in a neighbouring topic and the Unclassified bucket absorbs the rest. The projections are a study allocation, not a prediction of which questions will be set.
