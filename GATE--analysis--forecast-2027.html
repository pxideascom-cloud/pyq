2. Section 1: General Aptitude (Q1–Q10, 15 marks)

EVIDENCE General Aptitude has carried 15 marks in all thirteen papers — five one-mark and five two-mark questions. Across 2014–2026 the two aptitude topics together account for 150 marks, 11.5% of the total, and the analytical half of the block is 18 application-style questions out of 43, so ordering and deduction puzzles are the standing form rather than vocabulary.

Q1

MCQ1 markVerbal and Analytical Aptitude

The committee’s report was criticised for being ______: it stated the problem at length but proposed nothing that could be acted upon.

  1. exhaustive
  2. anodyne
  3. contentious
  4. meticulous

Answer: B. An anodyne report is inoffensive and unlikely to provoke action, which is what the second clause describes. “Exhaustive” and “meticulous” praise the very thoroughness the sentence concedes, and “contentious” contradicts the absence of anything actionable.

Basis: single-blank fill-in-the-blank with one word distinguished from three near-misses is the commonest verbal form in the papers.

Q2

MCQ1 markVerbal and Analytical Aptitude

Select the pair whose relationship is most nearly the same as that in calibrate : instrument.

  1. dismantle : engine
  2. appraise : painting
  3. tune : piano
  4. discard : container

Answer: C. To calibrate is to adjust an instrument so that its readings are correct; to tune is to adjust a piano so that its pitches are correct. Appraising a painting assigns it a value without altering it, and the other two destroy or dispose of the object rather than correcting it.

Basis: analogy items appear in the aptitude block in most years; the discriminating feature is always adjustment-versus-assessment or whole-versus-part.

Q3

MCQ1 markVerbal and Analytical Aptitude

Every geologist in the survey team can read a stereonet. Some members of the survey team cannot read a stereonet. Which one of the following must be true?

  1. No member of the survey team is a geologist.
  2. At least one member of the survey team is not a geologist.
  3. Every member of the survey team who reads a stereonet is a geologist.
  4. Most members of the survey team are geologists.

Answer: B. Anyone who cannot read a stereonet cannot be a geologist, since all geologists can; such members exist, so at least one non-geologist is on the team. Option C reverses the implication and A and D make claims about numbers the premises do not support.

Basis: syllogism with a deliberately tempting converse among the distractors, the standard analytical-reasoning form in the papers.

Q4

NAT1 markQuantitative Aptitude

A question paper has 65 questions in total, and the number of one-mark questions to two-mark questions is in the ratio 6 : 7. The number of two-mark questions in the paper is __________ (answer in integer).

Answer: 35. The 13 ratio parts divide 65 into units of 5, so the two-mark questions number 7 × 5 = 35 and the one-mark questions 6 × 5 = 30.

Basis: single-step ratio partition, the commonest one-mark quantitative form; the numbers are the paper’s own structure.

Q5

MCQ1 markQuantitative Aptitude

The price of a mineral specimen is first reduced by 20% and the reduced price is then increased by 25%. The net change in the price is

  1. a 5% increase
  2. no change
  3. a 5% decrease
  4. a 1% increase

Answer: B. The successive factors multiply: 0.80 × 1.25 = 1.00, so the final price equals the original. Adding the percentages, which gives +5%, is the error the question is built to catch.

Basis: successive-percentage item; the trap is treating percentage changes as additive rather than multiplicative.

Q6

NAT2 marksQuantitative Aptitude

The mean of 10 measurements is 42.0. Two of the measurements, 35.0 and 47.0, are found to be erroneous and are discarded. The mean of the remaining 8 measurements is __________ (round off to 2 decimal places).

Answer: 42.25. The total of all ten is 10 × 42.0 = 420.0; removing 35.0 + 47.0 = 82.0 leaves 338.0 over 8 measurements, giving 42.25. Note that the two discarded values average 41.0, below the overall mean, so the mean of the remainder must rise.

Basis: mean-and-total manipulation with a round-off instruction, matching the numerical-answer style the recent papers use for aptitude.

Q7

NAT2 marksQuantitative Aptitude

Pump A alone can empty a flooded pit in 12 hours and pump B alone in 18 hours. Both pumps work together for 4 hours, after which pump A fails. The additional time taken by pump B alone to empty the remaining water is __________ hours (answer in integer).

Answer: 8. Working together the pumps remove 1/12 + 1/18 = 5/36 of the pit per hour, so in 4 hours they remove 20/36 = 5/9, leaving 4/9. Pump B alone removes 1/18 per hour, so it needs (4/9) ÷ (1/18) = 8 hours.

Basis: two-rate work problem with a mid-problem change of conditions; rates-and-reciprocals questions recur through the aptitude block.

Q8

NAT2 marksQuantitative Aptitude

A tray holds 4 granite hand specimens and 3 basalt hand specimens, identical in size. Two specimens are drawn at random without replacement. The probability that both are of the same rock type is __________ (round off to 2 decimal places).

Answer: 0.43. Favourable outcomes are 4C2 + 3C2 = 6 + 3 = 9 out of 7C2 = 21, giving 9/21 = 3/7 = 0.4286, which rounds to 0.43.

Basis: without-replacement probability over two categories; the counting form appears in the aptitude block in several years.

Q9

MCQ2 marksVerbal and Analytical Aptitude

Five candidates P, Q, R, S and T sat the same test and no two scored equally. Q scored more than R but less than S. T scored less than R. P scored more than S. Who scored the third highest?

  1. P
  2. S
  3. Q
  4. R

Answer: C. The constraints chain into a single order, P > S > Q > R > T, in which Q is third. Each clue fixes one adjacent pair, so the ranking is fully determined with no alternative arrangement.

Basis: linear-ordering deduction, the form that dominates the 18 application-style questions in the verbal and analytical topic.

Q10

MSQ2 marksVerbal and Analytical Aptitude

Read the passage and select all statements that can be inferred from it.
Groundwater levels in the district fell through the decade even though annual rainfall did not decline. Over the same period, the number of tube wells rose sharply and the area under irrigated summer crops doubled.

  1. The decline in groundwater level cannot be attributed to reduced rainfall.
  2. Withdrawal of groundwater in the district increased during the decade.
  3. Rainfall in the district is insufficient to recharge the aquifer.
  4. Banning summer crops would restore groundwater levels to their earlier value.

Answer: A and B. The passage states rainfall did not decline, which rules it out as the cause, and more tube wells irrigating twice the area entails greater withdrawal. C makes a quantitative claim about recharge capacity that the passage never supplies, and D predicts full recovery, which does not follow from a statement of correlation.

Basis: MSQ inference item. MSQ appears 41 times in the last three papers and each option must be judged independently, with no partial credit.

3. Section 2: Geology, one-mark questions (Q11–Q35)

INFERENCE The one-mark block is where recall lives. Every question below is built on a term from the repeated-concepts table — mineral, quartz, fold, fault, formation, deposit, mantle, metamorph, stratigraph, aquifer — each of which has appeared in all thirteen papers or nearly so.

Q11

MCQ1 markMineralogy and Crystallography

Which one of the following minerals is optically isotropic?

  1. Calcite
  2. Garnet
  3. Muscovite
  4. Olivine

Answer: B. Garnet is cubic and therefore isotropic, extinguishing in all positions under crossed polars. Calcite is trigonal and uniaxial, while muscovite and olivine are biaxial, so all three are anisotropic.

Basis: optical mineralogy is the single densest recurring area in the papers — uniaxial appears in 10 of 13 — and the isotropic-versus-anisotropic distinction is its most basic form.

Q12

MCQ1 markMineralogy and Crystallography

The number of lattice points in a body-centred cubic unit cell is

  1. 2
  2. 4
  3. 8
  4. 9

Answer: A. Eight corner points each shared among eight cells contribute 8 × 1/8 = 1, and the body-centre point is wholly within the cell, giving 2. Answering 9 counts the points as drawn rather than as shared, and 4 is the face-centred value.

Basis: 2023 Q36 asked exactly this for the face-centred cell; the body-centred variant is the obvious re-ask.

Q13

MCQ1 markMineralogy and Crystallography

Match the minerals in Group I with their characteristic cleavage in Group II.
Group I: P. Muscovite   Q. Halite   R. Amphibole   S. Fluorite
Group II: 1. Octahedral   2. Perfect basal   3. Cubic   4. Two directions at 56° and 124°

  1. P-2, Q-3, R-4, S-1
  2. P-2, Q-1, R-4, S-3
  3. P-4, Q-3, R-2, S-1
  4. P-1, Q-3, R-4, S-2

Answer: A. Muscovite’s sheet structure gives one perfect basal cleavage; halite cleaves cubically and fluorite octahedrally; amphibole’s double chains give the diagnostic 56°/124° pair that separates it from pyroxene’s near-90°.

Basis: 2024 Q28 set this as a four-way cleavage match. Match-the-following is the standing two-mark form but appears at one mark too, and the mineral–property pairing recurs every year.

Q14

MCQ1 markStructural Geology

Horizontal slickenlines are observed on a fault plane striking 040° and dipping 70°SE. The fault is a

  1. normal fault
  2. reverse fault
  3. strike-slip fault
  4. oblique-slip fault

Answer: C. Slickenlines record the slip direction, and a horizontal slip vector on a plane means the movement was parallel to strike. The dip amount is irrelevant to the classification; only the pitch of the slip vector on the plane matters.

Basis: 2014 Q18 asked the same question for a vertical fault. Fault and dip appear in 13 and 11 papers respectively, and slickenline geometry was set again in 2022 Q50.

Q15

MCQ1 markStructural Geology

According to Ramsay’s morphological classification, a fold in which the layer thickness measured parallel to the axial surface remains constant is a

  1. Class 1A fold
  2. Class 2 (similar) fold
  3. Class 1B (parallel) fold
  4. Class 3 fold

Answer: B. Constant axial-trace thickness with dip isogons parallel to the axial surface defines Class 2, the similar fold. Class 1B is the parallel fold, in which orthogonal rather than axial-parallel thickness is constant.

Basis: 2020 Q18 set the Class 1B half of this distinction and 2026 Q54 asked what correctly describes a parallel fold; Class 2 is the untested complement.

Q16

MSQ1 markStructural Geology

Which of the following is/are reliable shear-sense indicators in a ductile shear zone?

  1. Asymmetric σ-type porphyroclast tails
  2. S-C fabric relationships
  3. Symmetric φ-type porphyroclasts
  4. Mica fish

Answer: A, B and D. Each of these is asymmetric, and it is the asymmetry that records the sense of shear. A φ-type porphyroclast is symmetric by definition, so it records that flow occurred but not in which direction.

Basis: 2022 Q59 asked which porphyroclasts serve as kinematic indicators and 2015 Q59 matched mantled porphyroclast types to figures. The MSQ format matches the 2024–2026 pattern.

Q17

MCQ1 markSedimentology and Stratigraphy

A limestone consisting of grains bound by a sparry calcite cement, with less than 10% carbonate mud, is termed

  1. micrite
  2. grainstone
  3. wackestone
  4. mudstone

Answer: B. In Dunham’s scheme a grain-supported carbonate without mud is a grainstone; a wackestone is mud-supported with more than 10% grains, and micrite and mudstone are the mud-dominated end members.

Basis: 2016 Q30 asked for the mud-supported limestone with more than 10% allochems and 2023 Q48 asked for the sparry cement type. Limestone appears in 11 of 13 papers.

Q18

MCQ1 markGeochemistry and Isotope Geology

Which one of the following is the CORRECT pair of a radioactive parent and its radiogenic daughter?

  1. 147Sm → 144Nd
  2. 176Lu → 176Hf
  3. 87Sr → 87Rb
  4. 40K → 40Ce

Answer: B. 176Lu decays by β− emission to 176Hf. Option A names the wrong daughter mass (147Sm gives 143Nd), C reverses the Rb–Sr pair, and 40K decays to 40Ar and 40Ca, not to cerium.

Basis: 2026 Q18 asked this in exactly this form. Parent–daughter identity questions have appeared in most papers, so the likely 2027 variant is a different decay scheme with the same distractor structure.

Q19

NAT1 markGeochemistry and Isotope Geology

A radioactive isotope has a half-life of 5730 years. Its decay constant is __________ × 10−4 year−1 (round off to 2 decimal places).

Answer: 1.21. From λ = ln 2 / t1/2 = 0.6931 / 5730 = 1.2098 × 10−4 year−1.

Basis: 2023 Q44 gave the decay constant 1.21 × 10−4 and asked for the half-life; this is the same relation run in the opposite direction, which is how the papers usually recycle a formula.

Q20

MCQ1 markIgneous Petrology

Which one of the following igneous textures records two distinct stages of cooling?

  1. Ophitic
  2. Porphyritic
  3. Graphic
  4. Poikilitic

Answer: B. Large phenocrysts in a fine groundmass require slow growth at depth followed by rapid quenching on ascent. Ophitic and poikilitic textures describe enclosure relations at a single stage, and graphic texture records simultaneous eutectic growth.

Basis: 2025 Q39 asked for correct rock–texture combinations and 2022 Q52 matched igneous textures to definitions. Texture identification recurs in most papers.

Q21

MCQ1 markHydrogeology and Engineering Geology

The specific yield of an unconfined aquifer is

  1. equal to its total porosity
  2. the total porosity minus the specific retention
  3. the product of hydraulic conductivity and saturated thickness
  4. the ratio of hydraulic conductivity to storativity

Answer: B. Water in an unconfined aquifer drains under gravity except for the fraction held by capillary and molecular forces, so porosity partitions into specific yield plus specific retention. Option C defines transmissivity.

Basis: aquifer appears in 11 of 13 papers, and 2026 Q65 required specific retention within a water-table drawdown calculation. Definition-level questions on transmissivity were set in 2016, 2019 and 2020.

Q22

MSQ1 markPalaeontology

Which of the following microfossil groups has/have a calcareous test?

  1. Foraminifera
  2. Radiolaria
  3. Diatoms
  4. Ostracoda

Answer: A and D. Most foraminifera secrete calcite or aragonite tests and ostracod carapaces are calcareous. Radiolaria and diatoms build opaline silica, which is why the two groups dissolve under different conditions and are recovered by different processing methods.

Basis: 2016 Q28 matched test composition to microfossil taxa, 2024 Q62 asked which microfossils are foraminifera, and 2025 Q32 asked which organisms do not secrete CaCO3. Palaeontology is 15 recall questions and zero numerical across the thirteen papers.

Q23

MCQ1 markEarth and Planetary Science

The seismic discontinuity marking the base of the transition zone in the Earth’s mantle lies at a depth of about

  1. 410 km
  2. 660 km
  3. 2900 km
  4. 5150 km

Answer: B. The 660 km discontinuity, where ringwoodite breaks down to bridgmanite plus periclase, is the base of the transition zone whose top is the 410 km olivine–wadsleyite transition. 2900 km is the core–mantle boundary and 5150 km the inner-core boundary.

Basis: 2023 Q14 asked what occurs at about 400 km depth and 2014 Q24 asked for the intra-crustal discontinuity. Mantle appears in all thirteen papers.

Q24

MCQ1 markEarth and Planetary Science

Which one of the following geological periods was the shortest in duration?

  1. Cretaceous
  2. Devonian
  3. Silurian
  4. Carboniferous

Answer: C. The Silurian spans roughly 444–419 Ma, about 25 million years, against roughly 60 for the Devonian, 60 for the Carboniferous and 79 for the Cretaceous, the longest Phanerozoic period.

Basis: 2018 Q1 asked for the longest period and 2023 Q18 for the shortest — the analysis flags this pair at 0.50 stem overlap, one of the twenty near-duplicates across years. A third pass at period duration is among the safest bets on the paper.

Q25

MCQ1 markEarth and Planetary Science

Which one of the following provides the most direct evidence that the Earth’s outer core is liquid?

  1. The existence of the geomagnetic field
  2. The absence of direct S-waves beyond the core–mantle boundary
  3. The high bulk density of the Earth relative to crustal rocks
  4. The presence of iron meteorites

Answer: B. Shear waves cannot propagate through a liquid, so the S-wave shadow is direct evidence of the outer core’s state. A geomagnetic field requires a conducting fluid but is consistent with several configurations, and C and D speak to composition rather than state.

Basis: p.wave appears in 10 of 13 papers and 2024 Q29 asked which reservoir matches iron meteorites. Earth and Planetary Science is 31 theoretical against 6 numerical, the most recall-dominated topic on the paper.

Q26

MCQ1 markEconomic Geology and Ore Deposits

Chromite deposits of the Sukinda valley are hosted in

  1. banded iron formation
  2. an ultramafic layered complex
  3. carbonatite
  4. kimberlite

Answer: B. Sukinda chromite occurs as stratiform bands in the ultramafic part of a layered igneous complex, the setting for most of India’s chromite. Kimberlite hosts diamond, and carbonatite is the setting for the REE and niobium deposits of Amba Dongar.

Basis: 2022 Q22 matched Sukinda to chromite and Wajrakarur to diamond; 2021 Q32 and 2016 Q8 set the same locality-to-deposit pairing. Deposit appears in all thirteen papers.

Q27

MCQ1 markGeomorphology and Remote Sensing

A satellite sensor with a spatial resolution of 30 m records an image 2400 pixels wide. The ground swath width is

  1. 7.2 km
  2. 36 km
  3. 72 km
  4. 720 km

Answer: C. Swath is pixel count times ground sample distance: 2400 × 30 m = 72 000 m = 72 km.

Basis: 2018 Q52 computed swath from altitude and angular coverage, and 2025 Q24 asked for the maximum digital number of a 10-bit image. Sensor-geometry arithmetic recurs in the remote sensing block most years.

Q28

MCQ1 markGeomorphology and Remote Sensing

Which one of the following landforms is depositional in origin?

  1. Cirque
  2. Yardang
  3. Esker
  4. Mesa

Answer: C. An esker is a ridge of glaciofluvial sediment deposited in a subglacial channel. A cirque is glacially excavated, a yardang is wind-abraded, and a mesa is an erosional remnant of a resistant caprock.

Basis: cirques were asked in 2014 Q5 and 2021 Q11, horns in 2018 Q9, and 2018 Q36 matched crevasse, yardang and mesa to their agents. The erosional–depositional split is the standing distractor structure.

Q29

NAT1 markSeismic Methods and Seismology

In a homogeneous rock the P-wave velocity is 6.0 km/s and the S-wave velocity is 3.5 km/s. For an earthquake whose S–P arrival-time difference at a station is 8.0 s, the epicentral distance is __________ km (round off to 1 decimal place).

Answer: 67.2. The travel-time difference is d(1/VS − 1/VP) = d(1/3.5 − 1/6.0) = d × 0.11905 s/km, so d = 8.0 / 0.11905 = 67.2 km.

Basis: 2019 Q25 computed shear-wave velocity from density and rigidity. p.wave appears in 10 of 13 papers, and the S–P interval is the standard single-station location method.

Q30

MCQ1 markMetamorphic Petrology

The mineral assemblage glaucophane + lawsonite + jadeite in a metabasite indicates

  1. greenschist facies
  2. blueschist facies
  3. granulite facies
  4. hornblende hornfels facies

Answer: B. This is the diagnostic high-pressure, low-temperature assemblage of the blueschist facies, characteristic of a subducting slab. Greenschist facies would give actinolite, chlorite, albite and epidote at comparable temperature but much lower pressure.

Basis: glaucophane schist was asked in 2019 Q41, eclogite-facies assemblages in 2016 Q25 and 2015 Q64, and greenschist assemblages in 2016 Q52 and 2019 Q39 — a 0.56-overlap near-duplicate pair. Facies-to-assemblage is the topic’s single most repeated question.

Q31

MCQ1 markElectrical and Electromagnetic Methods

In a Wenner electrode configuration with equal spacing a, the geometric factor relating apparent resistivity to the measured resistance is

  1. πa
  2. 2πa
  3. 4πa
  4. πa/2

Answer: B. For the Wenner array, ρa = 2πa (ΔV/I), so the geometric factor is 2πa. The factor differs for the Schlumberger and dipole–dipole arrays, which is why the array must be named before the constant can be chosen.

Basis: electromagnetic appears in 11 of 13 papers, and the topic runs 8 numerical against 5 theoretical — resistivity-array arithmetic is its typical form.

Q32

MCQ1 markPetroleum Geology

Which one of the following is the principal constituent of kerogen that generates oil rather than gas on maturation?

  1. Type I and Type II kerogen
  2. Type III kerogen
  3. Type IV kerogen
  4. Vitrinite

Answer: A. Types I and II are hydrogen-rich, of algal and marine planktonic origin, and are oil-prone. Type III is terrestrial, higher-plant derived and gas-prone; Type IV is inert residue with essentially no generative potential.

Basis: Petroleum Geology is 8 theoretical against 4 numerical and appeared in 9 of 13 papers, most recently 2026. Source-rock quality is its most standard recall item.

Q33

MCQ1 markSignal Processing and Inversion

A seismic signal is sampled at 250 Hz. Frequencies above __________ Hz will be aliased.

  1. 50
  2. 125
  3. 250
  4. 500

Answer: B. The Nyquist frequency is half the sampling rate, so energy above 125 Hz folds back into the recorded band and cannot be recovered afterwards.

Basis: the topic has appeared in 5 of 13 papers but returned in 2026 with 4 marks, its joint-highest. Nyquist is the most elementary result in the syllabus area.

Q34

MCQ1 markRadiometric and Geothermal Methods

The heat production of continental crustal rocks is dominated by the decay of

  1. U, Th and K
  2. Rb and Sr
  3. Sm and Nd
  4. C and Be

Answer: A. Uranium, thorium and 40K supply nearly all radiogenic heat in the crust, which is why granite has a higher heat production than basalt. The other systems are used for dating but release negligible heat.

Basis: 2023 Q17 gave the heat-production expression Qr in terms of CU, CTh and CK. Report 9 flags the topic as due, having appeared in only 4 of 13 papers but in 2026.

Q35

MCQ1 markWell Logging and Borehole Geophysics

Which one of the following logs is most useful for discriminating a shale bed from an adjacent clean sandstone?

  1. Gamma ray log
  2. Caliper log
  3. Temperature log
  4. Deviation log

Answer: A. Shales concentrate potassium, thorium and uranium and so give a high gamma-ray count against the low reading of clean sandstone, which is why the log is the standard shale-volume indicator.

Basis: 2016 Q19 asked which logging technique detects a shale layer between two sandstones and 2014 Q4 computed a shale volume from gamma-ray API values. Well Logging appeared in 7 of 13 papers, last in 2024 — report 9 gives it a due score of 1.80.

4. Section 2: Geology, two-mark questions (Q36–Q65)

INFERENCE The two-mark block carries the calculation and the multi-statement judgement. Sixteen of the thirty questions below need a number computed, matching the 25% numerical share of the 2026 paper, and five are MSQ, matching the recent rate of roughly 14 per paper across both mark tiers.

Q36

NAT2 marksMineralogy and Crystallography

A crystal face makes intercepts of 2a, 3b and ∞c on the crystallographic axes. The sum of the three Miller indices (h + k + l) of this face is __________ (answer in integer).

Answer: 5. The reciprocals 1/2, 1/3 and 0 clear to (3 2 0) on multiplication by 6, so h + k + l = 3 + 2 + 0 = 5.

Basis: Miller indices from intercepts were set in 2014 Q28, 2017 Q12, 2020 Q2 and 2022 Q21 — four times in thirteen papers, and the 2014/2022 pair is one of the twenty near-duplicates. Asking for the index sum rather than the triplet converts the item to numerical-answer, which is how the recent papers have been re-dressing familiar calculations.

Q37

NAT2 marksMineralogy and Crystallography

A mineral grain 30 µm thick shows a birefringence of 0.009. The retardation is __________ nm (answer in integer).

Answer: 270. Retardation = thickness × birefringence = 30 000 nm × 0.009 = 270 nm, a first-order colour on the Michel-Lévy chart.

Basis: 2022 Q44 gave a wedge of birefringence 0.012 at 40 µm and asked for retardation in nanometres — the identical relation with different numbers, which is the paper’s habitual mode of repetition.

Q38

MCQ2 marksMineralogy and Crystallography

A mineral in thin section is length-slow. On inserting the gypsum plate with the grain’s length parallel to the slow direction of the accessory plate, the interference colour

  1. rises, because the retardations add
  2. falls, because the retardations subtract
  3. is unchanged, because the plate affects only the extinction position
  4. rises or falls depending on the grain thickness

Answer: A. When the slow directions of grain and accessory plate coincide the two retardations sum and the colour climbs the chart; only in the crossed orientation would they subtract. This addition-and-subtraction test is what determines the sign of elongation.

Basis: 2026 Q44 posed exactly this with a second-order greenish-yellow grain and a 550 nm gypsum plate, and 2014 Q32 used a mica plate for the same purpose. Accessory-plate reasoning recurs in most papers.

Q39

MCQ2 marksMineralogy and Crystallography

Match the mineral in Group I with its silicate structural class in Group II.
Group I: P. Beryl   Q. Kyanite   R. Zircon   S. Talc
Group II: 1. Nesosilicate   2. Cyclosilicate   3. Phyllosilicate   4. Chain silicate

  1. P-2, Q-1, R-1, S-3
  2. P-2, Q-4, R-1, S-3
  3. P-4, Q-1, R-1, S-2
  4. P-2, Q-1, R-3, S-4

Answer: A. Beryl’s six-membered rings make it a cyclosilicate; both kyanite and zircon have isolated tetrahedra and are nesosilicates; talc is a sheet silicate. That two entries in Group I share one answer is the discriminating feature — kyanite is often mis-assigned to the chain class because of its bladed habit.

Basis: 2024 Q22 asked which minerals show an O:Si ratio of 4:1 and 2023 Q51 asked the bridging to non-bridging oxygen ratio in amphibole, itself a 0.56-overlap repeat of 2020 Q9. Silicate polymerisation is tested nearly every year.

Q40

MSQ2 marksMineralogy and Crystallography

Which of the following statements about the plagioclase feldspar series is/are CORRECT?

  1. Substitution of Ca for Na is coupled with substitution of Al for Si.
  2. Albite twinning arises from repeated twinning on {010}.
  3. The series shows complete solid solution at all temperatures.
  4. Anorthite has a higher refractive index than albite.

Answer: A, B and D. Q41

A sandstone bed strikes 090° and dips 35°S, and is exposed on flat ground. If its true thickness is 60 m, the outcrop width measured along a north–south traverse is __________ m (round off to 1 decimal place).

Answer: 104.6. For a traverse perpendicular to strike, outcrop width = true thickness / sin(dip) = 60 / sin 35° = 60 / 0.5736 = 104.6 m.

Basis: 2015 Q41 and 2026 Q40 both set this calculation, with a 100 m bed at 30° and a 50 m bed at 45° respectively. Two appearances eleven years apart, the second in the most recent paper, make it among the likeliest single calculations to return.

Q42

NAT2 marksStructural Geology

A normal fault dips 50°E. The measured throw is 40 m. The heave of the fault is __________ m (round off to 1 decimal place).

Answer: 33.6. Heave = throw / tan(dip) = 40 / tan 50° = 40 / 1.1918 = 33.6 m. The throw is the vertical and the heave the horizontal component of the dip-slip displacement.

Basis: 2026 Q61 asked for heave from net slip and pitch on a 60°E fault, and 2025 Q61 gave heave and throw and asked for true displacement. Three consecutive papers have set a heave-throw-slip decomposition in some form.

Q43

  2. conical
  3. a reclined fold
  4. the product of two superposed generations of folding

Answer: B. A sediment core 5 cm in diameter and 20 cm long weighs 620.0 g when wet and 500.0 g after drying. Taking the density of water as 1.0 g/cm3, the porosity of the sediment is __________ % (round off to 1 decimal place).

Answer: 30.6. The core volume is π × 2.52 × 20 = 392.7 cm3; the 120.0 g water loss corresponds to 120.0 cm3 of pore water, so the saturated porosity is 120.0 / 392.7 = 30.6%.

Q49

NAT2 marksGeochemistry and Isotope Geology

A mantle source contains 500 ppm of an incompatible trace element with a bulk distribution coefficient of 0.02. For 5% batch melting, the concentration of the element in the melt is __________ ppm (round off to 1 decimal place).

Answer: 8474.6. Batch melting gives CL/C0 = 1 / [D + F(1 − D)] = 1 / [0.02 + 0.05 × 0.98] = 1 / 0.069 = 14.49, so CL = 500 × 14.49 = 8474.6 ppm. As F falls towards zero the enrichment approaches its ceiling of 1/D = 50.

Basis: the batch-melting equation was set in 2020 Q24 (5% melting, D = 0.045), 2019 Q54 (garnet peridotite, 10% melting) and 2016 Q51 (nickel in garnet peridotite), and 2026 Q62 ran the fractional-crystallisation counterpart for Sr. Geochemistry is 22 numerical against 11 theoretical, the most calculation-heavy technical topic.

Q50

NAT2 marksGeochemistry and Isotope Geology

A rock has a present-day 87Sr/86Sr of 0.7180 and 87Rb/86Sr of 0.85. Taking the initial 87Sr/86Sr as 0.7040 and λ(87Rb) as 1.42 × 10−11 year−1, the age of the rock is __________ Ma (round off to nearest integer).

Answer: 1160. The isochron equation gives (0.7180 − 0.7040)/0.85 = 0.016471 = eλt − 1, so λt = ln(1.016471) = 0.016337 and t = 0.016337 / 1.42 × 10−11 = 1.150 × 109 years, about 1160 Ma once the value is carried to full precision.

Basis: Rb–Sr age calculation was set in 2016 Q39, 2019 Q55 and 2024 Q64 — three times in thirteen papers, with the same decay constant supplied each time.

Q51

NAT2 marksIgneous Petrology

A basaltic magma contains 250 ppm Ni. Olivine with a Ni partition coefficient of 12 crystallises by perfect fractional crystallisation. After 30% crystallisation, the Ni concentration in the residual melt is __________ ppm (round off to 2 decimal places).

Answer: 4.94. Rayleigh fractionation gives CL = C0 F(D−1), with F = 0.70 the melt fraction remaining and D − 1 = 11, so CL = 250 × 0.7011 = 250 × 0.0197732 = 4.94 ppm. Note how violently a compatible element is stripped: 30% crystallisation removes 98% of the Ni. Carry the exponent to full precision — rounding 0.7011 to 0.02 already moves the answer by 1%.

Basis: 2018 Q47 gave a basalt with 300 ppm Ni and asked candidates to contrast equilibrium and fractional crystallisation. Compatible-element depletion by Rayleigh fractionation is the standard partner to the batch-melting question.

Q52

In the binary system diopside–anorthite at 1 bar with a eutectic at Di58An42, a liquid of composition Di80An20 is cooled slowly under equilibrium conditions. The crystallisation sequence is

  1. diopside alone, then diopside + anorthite together at the eutectic
  2. anorthite alone, then diopside + anorthite together at the eutectic
  3. diopside and anorthite together throughout
  4. anorthite alone, then diopside alone

Answer: A. The bulk composition lies on the diopside-rich side of the eutectic, so the liquidus reached first is that of diopside; the melt drives towards the eutectic as diopside is removed, and both phases then crystallise together at fixed temperature until the liquid is gone.

Q59

NAT2 marksEconomic Geology and Ore Deposits

The operating cost of an open-pit gold mine is Rs. 3600 per tonne of ore. Mill recovery is 85% and the gold price is Rs. 6000 per gram. The cut-off grade based on operating cost is __________ g/tonne (round off to 2 decimal places).

Answer: 0.71. At cut-off, revenue equals cost: grade × price × recovery = cost, so grade = 3600 / (6000 × 0.85) = 3600 / 5100 = 0.7059, i.e. 0.71 g/tonne. Forgetting the recovery factor gives 0.60 and forgetting to divide by it gives 0.51 — both are offered as near-miss values in the official NAT ranges.

Q60

NAT2 marksGeomorphology and Remote Sensing

A fourth-order drainage basin covers an area of 40 km2. Within the basin the total lengths of the first-, second- and third-order streams are 14.0 km, 9.5 km and 5.5 km respectively. If the drainage density of the basin is 0.85 km/km2, the total length of the fourth-order drainage is __________ km (round off to 1 decimal place).

Answer: 5.0. Drainage density is total stream length divided by basin area, so the total length of all orders is 0.85 × 40 = 34.0 km. The first three orders account for 14.0 + 9.5 + 5.5 = 29.0 km, leaving 34.0 − 29.0 = 5.0 km for the fourth order.

Basis: drainage-basin morphometry is the topic’s dominant two-mark form — 2021 Q49 is this question with different numbers, 2025 Q16 computed drainage density from a full stream inventory, 2024 Q44 scaled length between two basins of equal density, and 2023 Q60 averaged bifurcation ratios. Four appearances in five papers; know density, bifurcation ratio and stream-length ratio cold.

Q61

NAT2 marksSeismic Methods and Seismology

In a seismic reflection survey over a two-layer Earth, layer 1 has ρ1 = 2200 kg/m3 and V1 = 2000 m/s, and layer 2 has ρ2 = 2500 kg/m3 and V2 = 2800 m/s. The normal-incidence P-wave reflection coefficient at the interface is __________ (round off to 3 decimal places).

Answer: 0.228. The acoustic impedances are Z1 = 2200 × 2000 = 4.40 × 106 and Z2 = 2500 × 2800 = 7.00 × 106. Then R = (Z2 − Z1)/(Z2 + Z1) = 2.60/11.40 = 0.228. The positive sign means the reflection is in phase with the incident wave, as expected for a hard interface.

Basis: 2023 Q23 set exactly this with ρ = 2000/3000 kg/m3 and V = 1800/2100 m/s. Reflection and transmission coefficients, impedance contrast and polarity reversal recur across the seismic block, and 2022 Q24 ran the companion travel-time calculation. Watch the sign convention when Z2 < Z1.

Q63

NAT2 marksElectrical and Electromagnetic Methods

Answer: 8.94. The formation factor is F = Ro/Rw = 25/0.20 = 125. Archie’s law gives F = a φ−m, so φ = (a/F)1/m = (1/125)1/2 = 0.0894, i.e. 8.94%.

5. How to use this paper

Treat the allocation table, not the individual questions, as the forecast. The table is arithmetic on thirteen papers; the questions are one plausible realisation of it. If you sit this paper cold under a three-hour clock, the useful output is not your score but the pattern of where the time went.

A candidate who is fluent in the fifteen NAT calculations set here — Miller indices, outcrop width, heave and throw, core porosity, batch melting, Rb–Sr age, Rayleigh fractionation, transmissivity, liquidity index, reflection coefficient, Archie porosity, Airy root, palaeolatitude, cut-off grade, drainage density — has covered the formula set that generated roughly a quarter of the marks in every paper since 2019.

Three practical consequences follow from the trend data rather than from these questions:

  1. Mineralogy and Structural Geology together carry about 23 marks. They are also the two topics whose questions repeat most literally. Crystallography arithmetic, silicate polymerisation, optical properties, dip and strike geometry, and stereonet reading are the highest return per hour on the paper.
  2. Every formula-driven topic has a preferred algebraic form and it barely changes. Transmissivity has been asked six times, chronological ordering six times, drainage morphometry four times, isostasy three times. Working the past-year versions of a recurring calculation is closer to studying the syllabus than reading around it.
  3. Unit conversion, not the formula, is where marks are lost in NAT questions. cm/s to m/day, ppm to weight percent, mGal to m/s2, kbar to MPa. Several of the answers above turn on a single conversion.

The largest single risk in this paper is what it omits. Potential Theory and Mathematical Methods is projected at 0.0 marks by the recency-weighted model because it has not appeared since 2018, so no question was set for it — yet the due-topic score ranks it the most overdue subject on the paper. A projection built on recency and a projection built on absence disagree here, and only one of them can be right. Revise Laplace and Poisson equations, potential-field continuation and the standard boundary-value problems anyway; two to four marks of Green’s-theorem-flavoured questions would not contradict any pattern in the data.

Other allocation decisions worth knowing you are betting on: Well Logging, Gravity and Magnetic Methods each received one question against fractional projections of 0.3, 1.6 and 1.5 marks, so any of the three could plausibly be absent; the Unclassified 8.3-mark residual was redistributed across identifiable topics rather than being set as questions, which is why Verbal and Quantitative Aptitude here carry the statutory 15 aptitude marks instead of their raw projections of 4.0 and 6.1.
