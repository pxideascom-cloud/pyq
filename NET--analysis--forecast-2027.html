2. Part A: General Aptitude (Q1–Q20, 30 marks)

INFERENCE Twenty questions, 2 marks each, of which the best fifteen count — so five can be abandoned without cost. A wrong answer costs 0.5, a quarter of the marks, which makes a blind guess among four options worth exactly zero and any elimination worth attempting. Sixteen of the twenty here are arithmetic and four are reasoning or data interpretation, matching the 79:21 split of the classifiable items in the record.

Q1

2 marksQuantitative

Tiwari and Deo together finish a job in 4 days, Deo and Hari in 6 days, and Hari and Tiwari in 12 days. Working alone, Tiwari would finish the job in

  1. 6 days
  2. 8 days
  3. 12 days
  4. 24 days

Answer: 3. Adding the three pair rates gives 2(T+D+H) = 1/4 + 1/6 + 1/12 = 1/2, so T+D+H = 1/4. Tiwari alone is the total minus the pair that excludes him: T = 1/4 − (D+H) = 1/4 − 1/6 = 1/12, i.e. 12 days. Choosing the right pair to subtract is the whole question.

Basis: 2016 June Q1 set exactly this shape — Tiwari and Deo in 2 hours, Tiwari and Hari in 3, Deo and Hari in 6 — and asked which statement about the individual times was incorrect. Pair-rate work problems appear in most Part A sets.

Q2

2 marksQuantitative

A, B, C and D are points on a circle, with AB = 5 cm, BC = 12 cm, AC = 13 cm and AD = 9 cm. The closest value of CD is

  1. 9.4 cm
  2. 10.0 cm
  3. 11.2 cm
  4. 12.0 cm

Answer: 1. Since 5² + 12² = 13², angle ABC is a right angle, so AC is a diameter. Then angle ADC is also a right angle and CD = √(13² − 9²) = √88 = 9.38 cm.

Basis: 2016 June Q5 gave the same 5–12–13 triangle inscribed in a circle with AD = 7 cm. Recognising the Pythagorean triple as a diameter marker is the step being tested, and it recurs.

Q3

2 marksQuantitative

How many integers in the set {1, 2, 3, …, 200} have an odd number of divisors?

  1. 10
  2. 14
  3. 20
  4. 28

Answer: 2. Only perfect squares have an odd divisor count, because divisors pair off except for the repeated square root. The squares up to 200 are 1² to 14² (196), so there are 14.

Basis: 2021 June Q11 asked this for {1, …, 100}, where the answer is 10. The set is the only thing that changes between years.

Q4

2 marksQuantitative

The digit in the unit's place of 7103 is

  1. 1
  2. 3
  3. 7
  4. 9

Answer: 2. Powers of 7 end in 7, 9, 3, 1 with period four. Since 103 = 4×25 + 3, the third term of the cycle applies, giving 3.

Basis: 2021 June Q2 asked for the unit digit of 48986 − 35163. Cyclicity of the last digit is the standard form of this item.

Q5

2 marksQuantitative

A shopkeeper buys 300 identical pens and sells the first 100 at 10% profit, the next 100 at 20% profit and the last 100 at 30% profit. If the total profit is Rs 3600, the cost of one pen is

  1. Rs 40
  2. Rs 60
  3. Rs 90
  4. Rs 120

Answer: 2. With cost c per pen the profit is 100c(0.10 + 0.20 + 0.30) = 60c = 3600, so c = Rs 60.

Basis: 2024 June Q5 set 200 watches sold in four blocks of 50 at 10, 20, 25 and 40% for a total profit of Rs 19 000. The block-profit form is a fixture of the recent papers.

Q6

2 marksQuantitative

Two vessels contain 1/3 L and 1/4 L of alcohol respectively. Water is added to each until both hold exactly 1 L, and the two litres are then mixed. In the mixture the ratio of alcohol to water is

  1. 7 : 24
  2. 7 : 17
  3. 1 : 2
  4. 1 : 3

Answer: 2. Alcohol totals 1/3 + 1/4 = 7/12 L in 2 L of mixture, so water is 2 − 7/12 = 17/12 L and the ratio is 7 : 17.

Basis: 2024 June Q4 is this question with different fractions. The trap is dividing alcohol by the total rather than by the water.

Q7

2 marksQuantitative

The sum of all the interior angles of a regular polygon is 1440°. The number of sides is

  1. 8
  2. 9
  3. 10
  4. 12

Answer: 3. The interior angles of an n-gon sum to (n − 2)×180°, so n − 2 = 8 and n = 10.

Basis: 2023 June Q15 asked for the sum of the interior angles of a regular octagon (1080°). The same identity, run in the other direction.

Q8

2 marksQuantitative

In a group of 2000 people, 600 read Hindi newspapers, 800 read English newspapers and 250 read both. The number who read neither is

  1. 600
  2. 850
  3. 1150
  4. 1400

Answer: 2. By inclusion–exclusion, readers of at least one = 600 + 800 − 250 = 1150, so 2000 − 1150 = 850 read neither.

Basis: 2021 June Q10 set this with 2000 people, 300 volleyball, 500 cricket and 200 both. The only variation between years is the numbers.

Q9

2 marksQuantitative

Three fair coins are tossed independently. The probability that at least two show heads is

  1. 1/8
  2. 3/8
  3. 1/2
  4. 5/8

Answer: 3. Of the eight equally likely outcomes, four have two or three heads (HHT, HTH, THH, HHH), giving 4/8 = 1/2.

Basis: 2023 June Q12 asked the probability that three thrown dice read the same, and 2021 June Q9 the probability that none of five students attempts a question. Elementary counting probability is asked most years.

Q10

2 marksQuantitative

The hour and minute hands of a clock coincide at 12:00. The next time they coincide is closest to

  1. 1 h 00 min
  2. 1 h 05 min
  3. 1 h 10 min
  4. 1 h 15 min

Answer: 2. The minute hand gains 360° − 30° = 330° per hour on the hour hand, so it regains a full lap in 12/11 h = 65 min 27 s.

Basis: 2020 June Q3 asked when the hands next become collinear after being in line. The 12/11-hour result is the whole of both questions.

Q11

2 marksQuantitative

A sheet 20 km long, 1 m wide and 0.05 mm thick is rolled tightly along its length into a solid cylinder of radius r and height 1 m. Ignoring air gaps, r is closest to

  1. 0.4 m
  2. 0.6 m
  3. 1.0 m
  4. 1.8 m

Answer: 2. The sheet's cross-sectional area is 20 000 m × 5×10−5 m = 1.0 m², and that becomes the circle's area: πr² = 1, r = 0.564 m.

Basis: 2021 June Q1 set a 10 km × 1 m × 0.1 mm sheet and asked for the cylinder's diameter. Conserving area rather than volume is the trick, and the units are where the marks go.

Q12

2 marksQuantitative

A hemispherical bowl of internal radius 9 cm is filled with liquid, which is poured into cylindrical bottles of radius 1.5 cm and height 4 cm. The number of bottles filled is

  1. 27
  2. 54
  3. 72
  4. 108

Answer: 2. Bowl volume = (2/3)π(9)³ = 486π cm³; bottle volume = π(1.5)²(4) = 9π cm³. The quotient is 54.

Basis: 2020 June Q2 set an idol of density 2 g/cm³ immersed in a well of 30 m² surface area and asked for its mass from the 10 cm rise. Volume-conservation arithmetic in one form or another appears in every Part A set.

Q13

2 marksQuantitative

A person invests a sum of money. Each time the amount doubles he donates Rs 500. After three such donations he is left with Rs 500. The amount he began with was

  1. Rs 375
  2. Rs 437.50
  3. Rs 500
  4. Rs 625

Answer: 3. Each cycle is x → 2x − 500, so after three cycles the amount is 8x − 3500. Setting that to 500 gives 8x = 4000 and x = Rs 500. Worth noticing: whenever the donation and the final balance are equal, the starting amount equals both, whatever the number of cycles — which is a two-second check if you spot it and a page of arithmetic if you do not.

Basis: 2020 June Q16 set exactly this with four donations of Rs 200 and Rs 200 remaining, where the same identity gives Rs 200. Running the recursion forwards is faster and safer than working backwards.

Q14

2 marksReasoning

Five students — P, Q, R, S and T — scored distinct marks. Q scored exactly the average of all five. R scored more than Q, S more than R, and P more than R. T scored the lowest. Which one of the following must be TRUE?

  1. P scored the highest
  2. S scored the highest
  3. Exactly one student scored below the average
  4. R scored the average of P and T

Answer: 3. The constraints force T < Q < R < {P, S}, so three students lie above the mean and only T lies below it. Which of P and S is highest is left undetermined, which is why options 1 and 2 both fail: the item rewards noticing what the constraints do not fix.

Basis: 2020 June Q5 gave five named students with the mean-scorer constraint and asked what followed. The step being tested is that a mean forces students onto both sides of it, and then counting how many the other constraints put on each side.

Q15

2 marksData interpretation

The bar chart of a country's medal tally shows totals of 26, 54, 64 and 101 in four successive games, with gold medals of 3, 10, 20 and 38. Which one of the following statements is NOT true?

  1. The gold tally rose whenever the total rose
  2. Non-gold medals were fewer than half the total in the last games
  3. The percentage rise in gold from the third to the fourth games exceeds that of the total
  4. Gold was under 40% of the total in every games

Answer: 2. In the last games non-gold = 101 − 38 = 63, which is 62% of the total — more than half, so the statement is false and is the one asked for. Gold rose 90% against the total's 58% between the third and fourth games, and gold peaks at 38/101 = 38%.

Basis: 2015 June Q14 set this chart with the same four-statement shape. The reliable approach is to compute the non-gold column before reading the options.

Q16

2 marksData interpretation

A scatter plot of two variables X and Y shows points falling almost exactly on a straight line of negative slope. The linear correlation coefficient is closest to

  1. 0
  2. +1
  3. −1
  4. −0.5

Answer: 3. A near-perfect straight-line relation gives |r| close to 1, and the sign follows the slope, so r ≈ −1. The magnitude measures scatter about the line, not the steepness of it.

Basis: 2021 June Q6 asked for the correlation coefficient nearest the scatter shown. Reading sign from slope and magnitude from scatter is the only skill involved.

Q17

2 marksQuantitative

A mountain road climbs three successive sections of equal horizontal length at slopes of 1 in 10, 1 in 20 and 1 in 30. The average slope of the whole climb is

  1. 1 in 15
  2. 1 in 16.4
  3. 1 in 20
  4. 1 in 60

Answer: 2. Over equal horizontal runs the rises add: for 1 unit of run each, total rise = 1/10 + 1/20 + 1/30 = 11/60 over 3 units, so the average slope is 11/180 = 1 in 16.4. Averaging the three slope denominators to get 1 in 20 is the trap.

Basis: 2012 June Q3 set a three-section mountain road and asked for the average slope of the entire climb. Weighting by run rather than averaging ratios is the whole question.

Q18

2 marksQuantitative

A regular solid satisfies F + V = E + 2, where F, V and E are its numbers of faces, vertices and edges. For four such solids, none touching another, the value of (F + V − E) summed over all four is

  1. 2
  2. 4
  3. 6
  4. 8

Answer: 4. Euler's relation gives F + V − E = 2 for each solid, so four separate solids give 4×2 = 8. The trap is treating the four as one object, which would give 2.

Basis: 2016 June Q3 set this for three distinct solids, answer 6. Only the count changes.

Q19

2 marksQuantitative

Abdul walks three times the distance Catherine walks, which is twice the distance Binoy walks. Catherine's speed is one third of Abdul's, which is twice Binoy's. If all three start together, who arrives first?

  1. Abdul
  2. Binoy
  3. Catherine
  4. All three together

Answer: 2. Take Binoy's distance as 1, so Catherine's is 2 and Abdul's is 6. Take Abdul's speed as 6, so Catherine's is 2 and Binoy's is 3. Times are 1, 1 and 1/3, so Binoy arrives first and the other two tie.

Basis: 2016 June Q2 is this question with the same ratios and the answer ‘both Abdul and Catherine’. Setting one quantity to 1 and scaling is faster than algebra.

Q20

2 marksReasoning

A sun at 45° elevation stands on one side of a vertical rectangular wall, which casts a rectangular shadow on level ground. The area of the shadow does NOT depend on the wall's

  1. length
  2. height
  3. thickness
  4. orientation to the sun

Answer: 3. At 45° the shadow extends a horizontal distance equal to the wall's height, so its area is length × height — the thickness enters only as a negligible strip. Length and height both scale it directly, and turning the wall relative to the sun changes the projected length.

Basis: 2021 June Q8 asked exactly this, with the same four dimensions offered. The reasoning is geometric rather than arithmetic, which is why it sits in the reasoning group.

Q21

2 marksAtmospheric Sciences and Climate

The dry adiabatic lapse rate of the atmosphere is closest to

  1. 6.5 K/km
  2. 9.8 K/km
  3. 5.0 K/km
  4. 2.0 K/km

Answer: 2. The dry rate is g/cp = 9.81/1004 K/m ≈ 9.8 K/km. 6.5 K/km is the environmental average, and confusing the two is what the item tests.

Basis: 2024 December (28 Feb) Q56 gave a saturated parcel rising through an environment at 6.5 K/km and asked about stability, and 2025 June Q52 asked when the atmosphere becomes conditionally unstable. The three lapse rates and their comparison are asked nearly every sitting.

Q22

2 marksAtmospheric Sciences and Climate

An air parcel is conditionally unstable when the environmental lapse rate lies

  1. above the dry adiabatic rate
  2. between the saturated and dry adiabatic rates
  3. below the saturated adiabatic rate
  4. exactly at the dry adiabatic rate

Answer: 2. Between the two adiabats the parcel is stable if unsaturated and unstable once saturated — the definition of conditional instability. Above the dry rate it is absolutely unstable and below the saturated rate absolutely stable.

Basis: 2025 June Q52 asked this directly and 2025 June Q60 asked which condition prevents air from rising freely. Stability criteria are the single most repeated atmospheric concept in Part B.

Q23

2 marksAtmospheric Sciences and Climate

Tropical cyclones rarely form within 5° of the equator because

  1. sea surface temperatures there are too low
  2. vertical wind shear is too strong
  3. the Coriolis parameter is too small to sustain rotation
  4. the troposphere is too shallow

Answer: 3. f = 2Ωsinφ vanishes at the equator, so a convergent disturbance cannot acquire the rotation a cyclone needs. Equatorial sea surface temperatures are in fact more than warm enough.

Basis: 2025 June Q53 asked exactly this. The Coriolis parameter's latitude dependence is asked in both parts and in several disguises — cyclogenesis, the Rossby radius, geostrophic balance.

Q24

2 marksAtmospheric Sciences and Climate

The southwesterly low-level flow over the Arabian Sea during the Indian summer monsoon is primarily driven by

  1. the cross-equatorial pressure gradient set up by continental heating
  2. the subtropical westerly jet
  3. local sea-breeze circulation along the Konkan coast
  4. Rossby wave breaking over the Bay of Bengal

Answer: 1. Summer heating of the Indian landmass lowers pressure over it; air crosses the equator and is turned right in the northern hemisphere into the southwesterly Somali jet. The subtropical westerly jet retreats north of the Himalaya before onset.

Basis: 2025 June Q58 asked this; monsoon appears in twelve of the thirteen years with 43 questions. The onset mechanism, the Somali jet and the tropical easterly jet are the three most frequent sub-topics.

Q25

2 marksAtmospheric Sciences and Climate

The spacing of isobars on a surface weather chart is a direct indication of the

  1. temperature gradient
  2. wind speed
  3. humidity
  4. cloud amount

Answer: 2. Closely spaced isobars mean a steep pressure gradient, and geostrophic wind speed is proportional to that gradient. Nothing about temperature or moisture follows from the spacing alone.

Basis: 2025 June Q56 asked precisely this. Isobar, isotherm and isohyet definitions have been asked since 2015 June Q35, which set them as a matching item.

Q26

2 marksAtmospheric Sciences and Climate

Turbulence in the atmospheric boundary layer over land at midday is generated primarily by

  1. wind shear alone
  2. radiative cooling
  3. wind shear together with surface buoyancy
  4. subsidence from aloft

Answer: 3. Mechanical shear is present whenever the wind blows over a rough surface, but midday heating adds a buoyant, convective source that usually dominates it — which is why the daytime boundary layer is deep and well mixed and the nocturnal one is thin and stably stratified.

Basis: 2025 June Q51 asked what causes boundary-layer turbulence, and 2024 December (28 Feb) Q125 asked for the Ekman-layer vertical velocity. The boundary layer is asked in both tiers, definitionally in B and as a calculation in C.

Q27

2 marksAtmospheric Sciences and Climate

Arrange these weather systems in order of decreasing horizontal scale: (i) tornado (ii) tropical cyclone (iii) thunderstorm cell (iv) mid-latitude depression

  1. (ii), (iv), (iii), (i)
  2. (iv), (ii), (iii), (i)
  3. (iv), (iii), (ii), (i)
  4. (ii), (iv), (i), (iii)

Answer: 2. A mid-latitude depression spans 1000–3000 km, a tropical cyclone 300–1000 km, a thunderstorm cell 1–10 km and a tornado under 1 km. The pair that decides the item is depression against cyclone: the cyclone is more intense but smaller.

Basis: 2025 June Q59 asked for the largest-to-smallest sequence of weather systems. Sequence-and-order items are 2.8% of Part B and this is their commonest atmospheric form.

Q28

2 marksAtmospheric Sciences and Climate

Rainwater formed by the condensation of evaporated seawater is, relative to that seawater,

  1. depleted in 18O and in dissolved salts
  2. enriched in 18O and depleted in salts
  3. depleted in 18O and enriched in salts
  4. identical in isotopic composition

Answer: 1. Evaporation preferentially removes the lighter isotopologue, so vapour and the rain formed from it are isotopically light; salts are non-volatile and do not enter the vapour at all. The distractors test whether the direction of the isotope fractionation is known, not merely that one exists.

Basis: 2025 June Q62 asked what rainwater from evaporated seawater contains relatively more or less of. Oxygen-isotope fractionation spans this topic and Geochemistry, and is asked in both.

Q29

2 marksAtmospheric Sciences and Climate

The restoring force for internal gravity waves in a stably stratified atmosphere is

  1. the Coriolis force
  2. buoyancy
  3. the pressure gradient force alone
  4. surface friction

Answer: 2. A displaced parcel in stable stratification is pushed back by the density contrast with its surroundings, and oscillates at the Brunt–Väisälä frequency. Coriolis is the restoring force for inertial and Rossby waves, not gravity waves.

Basis: 2024 December (28 Feb) Q60 asked exactly this. Distinguishing the restoring forces of gravity, inertial, Rossby and Kelvin waves is asked most years, often as the discriminator between four wave names.

Q30

2 marksAtmospheric Sciences and Climate

The Rossby radius of deformation for the atmosphere at 45°N, taking N = 10−2 s−1 and a scale height of 10 km, is closest to

  1. 300 km
  2. 1000 km
  3. 3000 km
  4. 10 000 km

Answer: 2. LR = NH/f with f = 2Ωsin45° = 1.03×10−4 s−1, so LR = (10−2×104)/1.03×10−4 ≈ 970 km. The magnitude, not the third digit, is what the options separate.

Basis: 2024 December (28 Feb) Q52 asked for the Rossby radius at 45°N with these same option magnitudes. Note that f at 45° is close to 10−4, which makes this calculation quick if you remember it.

Q31

2 marksOcean Sciences

With every 10 m increase in depth in the ocean, the pressure increases by about

  1. 10 mbar
  2. 100 mbar
  3. 1000 mbar
  4. 10 000 mbar

Answer: 3. ρgh = 1025 × 9.81 × 10 ≈ 1.0×105 Pa = 1 bar = 1000 mbar — roughly one atmosphere per 10 m, which is the number to carry.

Q32

2 marksOcean Sciences

The vertical profile of dissolved oxygen in the open ocean is approximately a mirror image of the profile of

  1. temperature
  2. salinity
  3. argon
  4. phosphate

Answer: 4. Respiration below the euphotic zone consumes oxygen and releases nutrients in a fixed stoichiometry, so the oxygen minimum coincides with the nutrient maximum. Argon is unreactive and tracks solubility, not biology.

Basis: 2025 June Q64 asked this. The coupling of oxygen to nutrients through respiration also underlies the Redfield-ratio item set as 2025 June Q66.

Q33

2 marksOcean Sciences

The slope of a regression of nitrate on phosphate concentration in the global ocean is closest to

  1. 1
  2. 6
  3. 16
  4. 106

Answer: 3. The Redfield ratio C:N:P = 106:16:1, so nitrate rises about 16 µmol/kg for every 1 µmol/kg of phosphate. 106 is the carbon term and is the intended distractor.

Basis: 2025 June Q66 set precisely this regression. Redfield stoichiometry is the most reliable single number in the ocean topic.

Q34

2 marksOcean Sciences

Ocean acidification driven by rising atmospheric CO2 most directly threatens

  1. squid
  2. bivalve shellfish
  3. demersal fish
  4. sea cucumbers

Answer: 2. Falling carbonate saturation makes calcium carbonate harder to precipitate and easier to dissolve, so calcifiers — bivalves, corals, coccolithophores, pteropods — are affected first. The other three are not primarily calcifiers.

Basis: 2025 June Q67 asked which group is most affected by acidification. Acidification, the carbonate compensation depth and the lysocline form a cluster asked in most recent sittings.

Q35

2 marksOcean Sciences

Satellite altimetry indicates that global mean sea level rose between 1993 and 2024 by approximately

  1. 25 cm
  2. 20 cm
  3. 16 cm
  4. 10 cm

Answer: 4. The altimetric record gives about 3.3 mm/yr over three decades, so roughly 10 cm in total. The distractors are what a candidate gets from a rate several times too large.

Basis: 2025 June Q39 asked this with these four options. Note the form — a current-observation question whose answer is a rate times a span, so the rate is what to memorise.

Q36

2 marksOcean Sciences

Coastal upwelling along the west coast of India during the southwest monsoon is driven by

  1. onshore Ekman transport under northerly winds
  2. offshore Ekman transport under equatorward alongshore winds
  3. tidal mixing over the shelf
  4. freshwater discharge from the Narmada and Tapi

Answer: 2. In the northern hemisphere Ekman transport is 90° to the right of the wind, so an equatorward alongshore wind drives surface water offshore and cold, nutrient-rich water rises to replace it. Reversing either the hemisphere or the wind direction gives downwelling.

Basis: upwelling is asked in ten of thirteen years, 23 questions. The Ekman-transport reasoning is the same one 2024 December (28 Feb) Q125 sets as a Part C calculation.

Q37

2 marksOcean Sciences

The Somali Current is distinguished among the world's western boundary currents by

  1. being the fastest
  2. reversing direction seasonally
  3. flowing only below the thermocline
  4. being driven by density rather than wind

Answer: 2. The monsoon reverses the wind field twice a year and the Somali Current reverses with it — northward and intense in the southwest monsoon, southward and weaker in the northeast. The Gulf Stream and Kuroshio are steady by comparison.

Basis: 2025 June Q142 set a matching item pairing the Somali Current with ‘seasonally reversing’. Ocean-current matching items recur, and the Somali Current is always the seasonal entry.

Q38

2 marksOcean Sciences

In the tropical open ocean the depth of maximum primary production lies above the depth of maximum chlorophyll because

  1. iron is more abundant at the surface
  2. dissolved inorganic carbon increases with depth
  3. light and nutrients are jointly optimal above the chlorophyll maximum
  4. grazing pressure is highest at depth

Answer: 3. Production needs both light, which falls with depth, and nutrients, which rise with it, so the rate peaks where the two overlap. Cells deeper down raise their pigment per cell to catch scarcer light, which puts the chlorophyll maximum below the production maximum.

Basis: 2025 June Q146 asked exactly this. The distinction between a rate maximum and a standing-stock maximum is what the item tests, and the same distinction underlies the compensation- and critical-depth items.

Q39

2 marksInterior of the Earth, Seismology and Geodynamics

A seismograph records the S-wave 90 s after the P-wave. Taking VP = 8.0 km/s and VS = 4.0 km/s, the epicentral distance is

  1. 360 km
  2. 720 km
  3. 1080 km

S-waves do not propagate through the outer core because it

Q43

2 marksInterior of the Earth, Seismology and Geodynamics

If the Earth kept its present average magnetisation but doubled in radius, the magnetic field at its surface would

  1. double
  2. quadruple
  3. stay the same
  4. fall to one eighth

Basis: 2014 December Q66 and 2015 December Q49 both set this, two sittings apart and in nearly identical words. It is among the most literally repeated items in the corpus.

If the Sun lost some of its mass, the length of the Earth's year would

  1. decrease
  2. increase
  3. stay the same
  4. depend on the Earth's own mass

A quartz silt grain of 0.01 mm diameter settles in still water at 20 °C. Taking ρs = 2650 kg/m³, ρw = 1000 kg/m³ and μ = 10−3 Pa·s, the Stokes settling velocity is closest to

  4. 9.0 mm/s

A sandstone has a mean grain size of 2φ. In millimetres this is

  2. 0.25 mm
  3. 2 mm
  4. 4 mm

  3. 17 m
  4. 15 m

  3. 32°
  4. 62°
