10. Preparation strategy

What to study, in order

INFERENCE Half of the projected marks sit in 5 topics and 80% in 10. That is the whole case for sequencing revision rather than covering the syllabus evenly.

  1. First half of the marks (5 topics): Mineralogy and Crystallography; Structural Geology; Sedimentology; Economic Geology and Ore Deposits; Igneous Petrology
  2. Next 30% (5 topics): Stratigraphy; Geodynamics and Earth Interior; Palaeontology; Metamorphic Petrology; Geochemistry and Isotope Geology
  3. Remaining 20% (7 topics): Geomorphology and Physical Geology; Mineral Exploration; Fuel Geology; Remote Sensing and Geoinformatics; Environmental Geology and Natural Hazards; Hydrogeology; Engineering Geology

How to answer, by stage

INFERENCE Prelims rewards precision over recall. 16% of items are statement sets or list-matching, both of which are scored all-or-nothing on three or four separate facts, and a further 6% turn on the word not. With a third of a mark deducted per wrong answer, the arithmetic of guessing is unforgiving but not uniform: on a four-option item a blind guess is worth (0.25 × 2.5) − (0.75 × 0.83) = −0.42 marks; eliminating one option makes it +0.28, and eliminating two makes it +0.83. So the rule is not ‘never guess’ but ‘never guess blind’, and the skill worth drilling is elimination.

EVIDENCE Mains rewards structure. 33% of the descriptive parts are 5-mark short notes, where a labelled sketch and four sentences earn as much as a page of prose; the 15- and 20-mark parts are where a diagram, a worked example and an Indian case study separate scripts.

Where this analysis is weakest

  • EVIDENCE No answer keys. UPSC publishes prelims keys separately and they are not in this collection, so no item here is marked with its correct option and no difficulty estimate is possible.
  • EVIDENCE OCR damage. Ten of the fourteen prelims papers are photocopies; 50 items lost enough text that they could not be classified, and some that were classified rest on a single surviving term.
  • INFERENCE Weightage of the paper, not of the script. Optional mains questions are counted at their printed weight even though a candidate answers barely half of them, so a topic that lives in optional questions looks heavier here than it is on any one answer sheet.
  • EVIDENCE One missing paper. The 2021 mains Paper III was never published, so 2021 contributes 700 weighted marks instead of 900 and every Paper III topic is slightly under-counted in that year.

What this implies

INFERENCE The three heaviest heads — Geography, Physics, Biology — carry 38 of the 100 marks between them, and 9 of the 10 named heads have appeared in every year on record. Science is the largest single block: physics, chemistry and biology together account for 35 marks a year against 23 for history, polity and economy combined.

INFERENCE The arithmetic of the paper differs from Paper II in one way that matters. An item is worth 0.83 marks and a wrong answer costs 0.28, so a blind guess is worth −0.14 marks against −0.42 in Paper II — the same proportion, a third the size. Guessing is no more attractive, but the cost of a mistake is smaller, and the paper is short enough that finishing is rarely the constraint.

EVIDENCE Unclassified is the third-largest row in the table, at 13 marks a year, and that is a limitation of this analysis rather than a feature of the paper. General Studies is open-ended by design — an item about a naval officer in the news or a newly named asteroid matches no syllabus head at all — and ten of the fourteen prelims papers are photocopies whose OCR leaves some stems too damaged to read. Treat the named rows as a floor on each head's weight, not an estimate of it.

SPECULATIVE No projection of individual General Studies topics is offered, and that is a considered omission rather than an oversight. Seven years is thin, the current-affairs head is unbounded by definition, and Paper I marks do not enter the final ranking — the paper is a qualifying gate. The defensible reading of the table above is that the science heads are the reliable ones and the place to spend preparation time; current affairs is the head where a candidate can lose marks without any way to have prepared for them.
